Canine Interface Guidelines

Interface guidelines for a user you cannot ask: a dichromat with 20/75 acuity whose input device is a nose.

Why not HIG

Human Interface Guidelines describe a human: a fingertip, a 44 pt target, red as warning, text as content. Every one of those assumptions breaks on our user, so in dog mode HIG are not merely insufficient — they are harmful. CIG states the laws for the dog; for the human we defer to HIG and record only the 2 places where we depart from it.

Every number and colour in a law comes from design/tokens.json and is interpolated at build time. The one exception is this document's own chrome palette, which is not a product value and is not in the tokens.

CIG-1

The user

A specification, not a description. Everything below follows from these 6 rows.

CIG-1.1

Dichromat: cones peaking at 429–435 nm and 555 nm. red and green are indistinguishable.

WhyThis is not a poorer palette. It is a differently shaped colour space.
CheckNo information the dog needs is encoded in a red–green opposition.
CIG-1.2

Acuity 20/75 (range 20/45–20/85), roughly 3.75× weaker than human.

WhyDetail does not exist. The carriers are silhouette, colour mass and motion.
CheckEvery dog-mode element clears the size floor in CIG-2.3.
CIG-1.3

Flicker fusion threshold 70–80 Hz, against ~60 Hz in humans.

WhyWhat the dog could see flickering is luminance modulation, not frame rate. On a sample-and-hold panel that is the dimming: this device uses PWM at 480 Hz at every brightness, which is six times the threshold, so it fuses.
CheckScreen dimming above 70–80 Hz: satisfied at 480 Hz. Room lighting is the open risk — a dimmed or mains-driven lamp can modulate below the threshold, and it is outside the app.
CIG-1.4

The input device is a nose.

WhyA wet nose is a large, moist, multi-point contact patch.
CheckNo interaction requires precision or a discrete tap; see CIG-3.
CIG-1.5

Contrast and size floors are set by Auri, aged 12, not the younger dog.

WhyIf it reads for her, it reads for both. The converse does not hold.
CheckTests run on both dogs; thresholds validated on the older.
CIG-1.6

The dog does not read an image as standing for a real dog.

WhyIt discriminates image features, but an image need not substitute for its real-world referent.
CheckThe product's output is a preference ranking, never a "match".

CIG-2

Perception

What may be shown, and how large it has to be.

CIG-2.1

Meaning is carried only by the blue ↔ acid opposition (#0033FF ↔ #D6F000). ink and white are figure and ground, not meanings.

WhyBoth hues sit near the peaks of canine cone sensitivity.
CheckThe neutral ramp (7 steps) appears in no signal role.
CIG-2.2

State must never be encoded in red or green.

WhyTo a dichromat both are grey, so the semantics do not exist.
CheckGrepping the dog-mode artboards finds neither hue.
CIG-2.3

Minimum dog-mode target 214×300 px. Type scale display 112 / name 80 / value 44.

WhyThe acuity factor is 3.75×, but the floors run higher still: 4.9× on target width, 6.8× on height, 4.3× on display type. The acuity number sets the direction, not the sizes.
CheckDecision zones measure 214×926 px — the full frame height.
CIG-2.4

Figure and ground separated by maximum contrast: #000000 against #FFFFFF, #D6F000 against #000000.

WhyContrast, not hue, is the dog's primary information channel.
CheckEvery signal survives conversion to greyscale.
CIG-2.5

120 Hz for motion continuity, not against flicker — flicker is CIG-1.3 and the dimming already clears it. Animate transform and opacity only.

WhyThe dog resolves change up to 70–80 Hz, so motion updated at 60 fps is steppier to it than to us. 60 fps is the platform default, not the hardware ceiling: iOS Safari caps page rendering near 60 fps by default, on ProMotion hardware too, to save battery. It is a platform default and not a hardware limit; the switch is Settings > Apps > Safari > Advanced > Feature Flags > "Prefer Page Rendering Updates near 60fps".
CheckNothing renders at 30 fps; no layout animation anywhere. Measure the achieved rate and record it — do not assume the panel's 120 Hz reaches the page.
CIG-2.6

Text is for the human only.

WhyThe dog does not read. A letterform cannot be the sole carrier of information.
CheckEvery dog-mode message has a non-text carrier: shape, motion or sound.

CIG-3

Input

Input is a patch, not a point — and a drag, not a tap.

CIG-3.1

The interaction primitive is a drag. Forbidden: tap, long-press, double-tap, pinch, edge swipe. One exception, CIG-3.6.

WhyCanine screen input naturally resembles a swipe and a drag. The Tinder gesture is, by coincidence, the right gesture.
CheckNo handler listens for a tap event in dog mode, outside D2.
CIG-3.2

The pointer is the centroid of the contact patch; touch count is ignored.

WhyA nose contact is an area, not a cursor, and the number of points is incidental.
CheckDecision logic never reads touches.length.
CIG-3.3

Drag threshold 40 px. Direction comes from the screen half: 2 zones of 214 px across the full 926 px height.

WhyA low threshold and enormous zones remove any demand for precision.
CheckAny drag longer than 40 px yields a decision.
CIG-3.4

After a decision, input is locked for 1500 ms.

WhyOne long nose drag must not register as three decisions.
CheckTwo decisions less than 1500 ms apart are impossible.
CIG-3.5

Undo is a core function, at human scale, reachable by the person sitting alongside.

WhyA wet nose misfires routinely. In Tinder and Bumble undo is a paid feature; here the mistake is the norm, not the exception.
CheckUndo is visible on D3 and reachable without leaving the session.
CIG-3.6

The only exception to the no-tap rule is D2.

WhyWarm-up calibrates the contact patch and builds the nose → consequence association, and only a contact can do that.
CheckThe exception is recorded in the tokens as input.tap.allowedOn and extends to no other screen.

CIG-4

Feedback

What happens after a decision — and where the loop actually closes.

CIG-4.1

Every "yes" does something immediately. No "maybe later" pile.

WhyThe lesson from the graveyard of swipe-for-jobs apps: a swipe that only defers an item saves nobody anything.
CheckA decision fires sound, the reward cue and the ranking entry in the same moment.
CIG-4.2

Sound is a primary carrier: bark 0.8 s, squeak 0.4 s, knock 0.2 s.

WhyIn a dog, hearing outranks vision — the more so at 20/75 acuity.
CheckEvery decision event has an assigned sound; levels come from the tokens.
CIG-4.3

Motion is information, not ornament. prefers-reduced-motion applies to human mode.

WhyIn dog mode motion carries meaning, so switching it off would remove content, not decoration.
CheckDisabling animation in dog mode is not implemented, and should not be.
CIG-4.4

The reward is physical. The screen announces the reward; it is not the reward.

WhyThe loop closes with a treat from the human, from a hand, in the room — not on the glass.
CheckD4 carries an explicit cue to the human that it is treat time.
CIG-4.5

A human present in the room is required.

WhyCanine engagement in an on-screen task rises with a person alongside.
CheckThe design admits no session in which the dog is left alone with the phone.

CIG-5

Session

The app ends. That is a decision, not a shortcut.

CIG-5.1

A deck holds 6–12 cards and it ends.

WhyThe floor is 6 because a rising decision time is the signal to shorten the deck.
CheckAfter the last card an end state appears, not another card.
CIG-5.2

No feed, no notifications, no streaks.

WhySwipe fatigue is a documented phenomenon: monetisation over purpose, repetitive labour instead of pleasure.
CheckThe code contains no notification schedule and no streak counter.
CIG-5.3

Success is measured by regularity and in-session engagement, not time in app.

WhyThe value to an ageing dog is cognitive stimulation, and that does not scale with session length.
CheckH1 reports decision times and the drop-off point, not total screen time.

CIG-6

The mode boundary

One law, but the product makes no sense without it.

CIG-6.1

The dog does not enter human mode. The transition gesture must be impossible for a nose — two simultaneous contact points, or a sequence.

WhyHuman mode holds data, profile editing and meeting approvals. A nose must not land there by accident.
CheckNo screen D1, D2, D3, D4, D5 contains a single target leading to H1, H2, H3, H4.
CIG-6.2

The end-of-session screen carries no "next" affordance at all.

WhyAn affordance the size of a nose is an affordance for a nose, whatever the intent behind it.
CheckD5 contains no bordered or filled rectangle at target dimensions.
CIG-6.3

At the OS level, iOS Guided Access closes the boundary.

WhyIt blocks the exit into Settings; CIG-6.1 governs the inside of the app.
CheckThe session procedure includes enabling Guided Access before the phone reaches the dog.

CIG-7

Platform layer

Without touch-action, overscroll-behavior, -webkit-user-select a nose dragged across the glass selects text, raises the magnifier loupe and scrolls the page.

CIG-7.1

Browser locks, on html and body:

touch-action: none;
overscroll-behavior: none;
-webkit-user-select: none;
user-select: none;
-webkit-touch-callout: none;
-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent;
WhyWithout touch-action, overscroll-behavior, -webkit-user-select a nose dragged across the glass selects text, raises the magnifier loupe and scrolls the page.
CheckDragging a finger across the screen on the device selects no text, raises no loupe and does not bounce the page.
CIG-7.2

Viewport width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover, user-scalable=no, manifest display: standalone, portrait, #000000.

WhyStandalone removes the Safari chrome; viewport-fit=cover hands us the full 428×926 px frame.
CheckAfter adding to the home screen no browser interface is visible.
CIG-7.3

Safe areas: 47 px top, 34 px bottom, via padding: env(safe-area-inset-top) env(safe-area-inset-right) env(safe-area-inset-bottom) env(safe-area-inset-left).

WhyThe real status bar and home indicator draw over our layout. We never paint a fake one.
CheckNo artboard paints a status bar; content stays clear of the home indicator.
CIG-7.4

Unlock audio on D2 warm-up.

WhyiOS will not play sound without a user gesture, and the dog's first touch is the only gesture before the decision loop.
CheckThe bark plays on the first "yes" of the session, not the second.
CIG-7.5

Profile video carries muted playsinline loop autoplay.

WhyiOS autoplays video only when muted and inline, so the clip's own audio track is irrelevant — sound plays separately from the unlocked AudioContext.
CheckVideo starts by itself on the device, with no gesture and no fullscreen.

CIG-8

Where HIG governs, not us

We are building a PWA, so Human Interface Guidelines do not formally bind us — Apple does not review a page added to the home screen. But in human mode HIG describes expectations, and those hold regardless of who enforces them. For H1–H2–H3–H4 we do not rewrite HIG: we defer to it and record only our 2 departures.

ConcernDog mode · D1–D5Human mode · H1–H4
Minimum targetCIG-2.3 — 214×300 pxHIG — 44×44 pt, adopted unchanged
Semantic colourCIG-2.2 — red and green forbidden as encodingsDeparture. HIG permits destructive red; we stay on blue ↔ acid so both modes read as one product
TypographyCIG-2.3 — display 112 pxDeparture. HIG assumes SF Pro and Dynamic Type; we use Anton and Barlow, because identity has to span both modes
GesturesCIG-3.1 — drag only, one exception (CIG-3.6)HIG — taps and standard gestures, adopted unchanged
Safe areasCIG-7.3 — 47 px and 34 pxHIG — identical
Reduced motionCIG-4.3 — not honoured; motion carries informationHIG — prefers-reduced-motion honoured
