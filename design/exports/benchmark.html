Two families, and the gap between them

Looking only at dog apps would have been a waste of a week. The market splits cleanly in two, and neither half does what this project does — so the second half of the benchmark leaves the category entirely.

Tinder for Chihuahua This project The dog operates, the human interprets. A preference ranking, which a human turns into a real meeting.
Two things worth the trip. App for Dog markets its buttons as “big, full-screen, so a curious nose can’t miss them” — the exact pattern this project derived from gesture morphology research, except somebody had already validated it commercially. And the founder story behind a Toronto “Tinder for dogs” is a man who built it because his older dog could not find anyone to play with. That is, more or less exactly, this brief. So the motivation is market-validated, and the differentiator has to be that here the dog does the swiping.

Part two · what dating and job apps already paid to find out

Pattern / sourceWhat it provesWhat this project takes
Tinder · the swipe The power of the gesture is a binary decision with zero interface chrome. Nothing stands between the content and the choice. The gesture and the empty screen. Here it is not styling: anything that is not the card or a decision zone costs the dog legibility.
Rewind / undo In human apps, undoing a swipe is a premium feature — a small mistake that can be monetised. Undo is core, not premium. A wet nose will misfire regularly, so it has to be large, immediate, and reachable by the human alongside.
Hinge Deliberately moved away from swiping towards prompts and comments, under the banner “designed to be deleted” — and grew revenue 22% doing it. Volume is not the goal. A small curated deck beats an infinite feed, and time-in-app cannot be the success metric.
Bumble Stated plainly: away from “optimising for swipe speed and volume”, towards fewer, better, more considered signals. The deck has a ceiling: 6 to 12 cards per session. That agrees with canine attention and with the cognitive-enrichment frame at the same time.
Double opt-in Consent from both sides — that mechanism, not the swipe itself, is what creates safety and makes a match worth anything. Rewritten for these two sides: the dog’s swipe is one consent, the human’s approval is the other.

A swipe that only moves something onto a “maybe later” pile has not saved anyone any work. It has relocated the decision — which is the diagnosis for the entire graveyard of “Tinder for jobs” apps, and the trap this project was walking into.
