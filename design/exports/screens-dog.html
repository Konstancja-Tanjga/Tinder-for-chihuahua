Tinder for Chihuahua · Chapter 10 · Dog mode

Five screens the dog sees

Four-times scale, no chrome, blue and yellow-green only. The strip along the top of the card screen is deliberately at human scale — the undo control and the deck counter belong to the person sitting alongside, and the difference in scale is what says so on the screen itself.

D1Choosing the dog

Two enormous tiles, recognisable without reading. This step is touched by the human, not the dog.

CIG-2.3

D2Warm-up

One large moving object; the whole screen is the target. Calibrates the contact patch, builds the nose → consequence association, and unlocks audio.

CIG-3.6 · CIG-7.4

D3Candidate card

The core screen. Muzzle video, two full-height decision zones, a directional chevron under each icon instead of a tap target.

CIG-3.1 · CIG-3.3

D4Reward cue

Not congratulations — an announcement. The bark plays and the human is told it is treat time. The loop closes off-screen.

CIG-4.4

D5End of session

Deliberately without a single acid pixel and without any "next" affordance. The absence of reward colour is the information.

CIG-5.1 · CIG-6.2

Look at D5. It is the only screen in the set with no acid anywhere and no bordered rectangle at target size. Both absences are deliberate: no reward colour means there is nothing left to win, and no target means a nose cannot leave dog mode. An affordance the size of a nose is an affordance for a nose, whatever the intent behind it.
