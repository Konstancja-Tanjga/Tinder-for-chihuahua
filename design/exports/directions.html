Tinder for Chihuahua · Chapter 08 · Visual directions

Three directions, one user who cannot tell you

The same three screens in each column — choose the dog, the candidate card, the reward — because comparing different screens would only compare my choice of screens. Each direction carries an argument for and an argument against, including the one that won. A direction with no named weakness has not been evaluated, only preferred.

ASoftrejected

Rounded corners, inset cards, a warm off-white ground on the reward screen. The direction that looks most like a normal consumer app.

Argument forThe friendliest to the human half of the audience, and the only one a stranger would call attractive at first glance.

Argument againstSoft edges are the first thing to dissolve at 20/75 acuity, and an inset card spends its contrast on a border rather than on the dog.

BGeometricrejected, one idea kept

A strict grid, thin rules, explicit numbers set in monospace beside every specification.

Argument forThe most legible about its own reasoning: every value is on the screen, which makes the spec and the design impossible to separate.

Argument againstThe grid spends its contrast on structure rather than on the figure. For a dichromat the lines compete with the thing that matters.

CPosterchosen

Enormous flat shapes, square corners, two hues on black, no chrome and no decoration anywhere.

Argument forThe highest figure-to-ground contrast of the three — which is precisely what a twelve-year-old dog’s raised contrast threshold demands.

Argument againstIn human mode the same language is shouting. H1–H4 needed their own scale rather than a shrunken copy of this one.

Why C won, and what B left behind. The deciding criterion was not taste but the highest available contrast between figure and ground, because that is what a twelve-year-old dog’s raised contrast threshold requires — and the personas had already settled that Auri sets the floor. One idea was carried over from B: explicit values in monospace next to the thing they govern, which is now how the token sheet reads. The two rejected directions keep their own palettes on the canvas and deliberately do not comply with the tokens, because they are a record of a decision rather than material to build from.
