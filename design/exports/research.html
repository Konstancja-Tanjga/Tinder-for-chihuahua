Tinder for Chihuahua · Chapter 02 · Research

Ten findings, ten requirements

You cannot ask this user anything, so the requirements have to come from the literature. Each finding below is paired with the constraint it imposes — the right-hand column is not commentary, it is the specification.

FindingWhat it requires
01
Colour vision

Two cone classes, peaking at 429–435 nm and 555 nm. Red and green are indistinguishable — not a poorer palette, a differently shaped colour space.

429–435 nm555 nmdichromat
Requirement

The palette is blue and yellow-green. Tinder's red-pink skin is ruled out functionally, not aesthetically. The touchscreen literature recommends exactly this pair.

02
Visual acuity

Miller and Murphy put a typical dog at 20/75; visual-evoked-potential work on beagles gives 20/45–20/85. The dog sees at 6 m what we see at 22.

20/7520/45–20/85~4×
Requirement

Roughly four times the human scale. Detail does not exist; the carriers are silhouette, colour mass and motion — never a typeface or a thin line.

03
Flicker fusion

The canine threshold sits around 70–80 Hz against ~60 Hz in humans. A screen refreshing 60 times a second, fluid to us, may visibly flicker to a dog.

70–80 Hz dog~60 Hz human120 Hz
Requirement

ProMotion stops being a luxury and becomes a hardware requirement. Animate transform and opacity only, so nothing drops to 60.

04
Gesture morphology

Canine screen input naturally resembles a swipe and a drag rather than a discrete, precise tap. This is the single most consequential finding in the set.

swipe > tap
Requirement

The Tinder gesture is, by coincidence, the right gesture — not a metaphor borrowed from a human app. Every drag counts, the distance threshold is low, direction comes from the screen half. No taps, no long-presses.

05
Physics of contact

A wet nose on capacitive glass is a large, moist, multi-point patch. It is not a cursor.

patch ≠ point1500 ms
Requirement

Treat input as an area: take the centroid, ignore the touch count, debounce, and lock input for 1500 ms after each decision so one long drag cannot register three. Undo is a core function, because the mistake is the norm.

06
Conspecific attention

Dogs look longer at dog faces than at human faces regardless of familiarity, and fixate primarily on the eye region.

muzzle > faceeyes
Requirement

The video frames the muzzle and eyes filling the shot. No full-body wides, no scenery, no people. A hard spec for the source clips, not a suggestion.

07
What a swipe means

A dog discriminates image features, but a two-dimensional image need not stand for its real-world referent — dogs recognise their owner far less well from a photograph than from a face.

preference signal
Requirement

The swipe is a real preference about what is on the screen, not a declaration about a mate. So the product's output is a ranking and session data, never a match. Hence two users, one screen.

08
Value to the older dog

The Messerli Research Institute in Vienna showed touchscreen games working as cognitive enrichment, aimed specifically at ageing dogs — they called it sudoku for dogs. Auri is twelve.

12 yearsenrichment
Requirement

A second, serious reason to exist beside a promise made to two dogs. It changes the success metric: what counts is regularity and in-session engagement, not card count.

09
Reward

Dogs work for food, and canine engagement in an on-screen task rises with a person present in the room.

physical rewardhuman present
Requirement

The screen is not the reward — it announces one. The loop closes in the room, with a treat from a hand. So we design an interaction where the human sits alongside rather than leaving the dog with a phone.

10
Age and contrast

In older dogs we expect reduced contrast sensitivity from changes in the lens. Auri and Karmel share the same dichromat baseline but not the same threshold.

older dog sets the floor
Requirement

Minimum contrast and minimum target size are set by the twelve-year-old, not the five-year-old. If it reads for her, it reads for both; the converse does not hold.

One honest caveat. Finding 10 is the only claim in this set not supported by a source in the bibliography. It is the most load-bearing assumption in the project — the whole “design for Auri” principle rests on it — so it is marked as an expectation to verify on the dog, not as a finding from the literature.
