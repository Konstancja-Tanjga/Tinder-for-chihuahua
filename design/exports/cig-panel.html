Canine Interface Guidelines

Human Interface Guidelines describe a human: a fingertip, a 44 pt target, red as warning, text as content. Every one of those assumptions breaks on our user, so in dog mode HIG are not merely insufficient — they are harmful. CIG states the laws for the dog; for the human we defer to HIG and record only the 2 places where we depart from it.

ink#000000 · ground
blue#0033FF · signal, near 429 nm
acid#D6F000 · signal, near 555 nm
white#FFFFFF · figure

A single source of truth

Every number and colour in a law comes from design/tokens.json and is interpolated at build time. The one exception is this document's own chrome palette, which is not a product value and is not in the tokens.

A review audit found the input cooldown restated in seven files and the palette hardcoded across ten artboards. The artboards are not fixed yet — this file exists so the restating stops spreading, and the build now refuses to publish a document containing an unresolved token or Polish text in the English version.

6 groups of laws

CIG-1The user6 laws

Dichromat: cones peaking at 429–435 nm and 555 nm. red and green are indistinguishable.

This is not a poorer palette. It is a differently shaped colour space.

CIG-2Perception6 laws

Meaning is carried only by the blue ↔ acid opposition (#0033FF ↔ #D6F000). ink and white are figure and ground, not meanings.

Both hues sit near the peaks of canine cone sensitivity.

CIG-3Input6 laws

The interaction primitive is a drag. Forbidden: tap, long-press, double-tap, pinch, edge swipe. One exception, CIG-3.6.

Canine screen input naturally resembles a swipe and a drag. The Tinder gesture is, by coincidence, the right gesture.

CIG-4Feedback5 laws

Every "yes" does something immediately. No "maybe later" pile.

The lesson from the graveyard of swipe-for-jobs apps: a swipe that only defers an item saves nobody anything.

CIG-5Session3 laws

A deck holds 6–12 cards and it ends.

The floor is 6 because a rising decision time is the signal to shorten the deck.

CIG-6The mode boundary3 laws

The dog does not enter human mode. The transition gesture must be impossible for a nose — two simultaneous contact points, or a sequence.

Human mode holds data, profile editing and meeting approvals. A nose must not land there by accident.

CIG-7 · Platform layer

touch-action: none;
overscroll-behavior: none;
-webkit-user-select: none;
user-select: none;
-webkit-touch-callout: none;
-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent;

Without the first 3 rules the input law is unsatisfiable. Without touch-action, overscroll-behavior, -webkit-user-select a nose dragged across the glass selects text, raises the magnifier loupe and scrolls the page. This is not an implementation detail — it is the precondition for a swipe reaching the app at all.

Two further iOS constraints shape the flow rather than the code: iOS will not play sound without a user gesture, and the dog's first touch is the only gesture before the decision loop, so D2 warm-up must unlock the AudioContext. And iOS autoplays video only when muted and inline, so the clip's own audio track is irrelevant — sound plays separately from the unlocked AudioContext.
