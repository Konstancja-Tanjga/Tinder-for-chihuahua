Tinder for Chihuahua · Chapter 03 · Personas

Two users on paws, one on two legs

The canine personas are described by sensory profile, motor behaviour and motivation, because none of that can be asked for. The human persona has a completely different structure, because her problem is not perception — it is role.

KarmelPrimary swiper — and the stress test for input
Age, sex
5 years, male
Sensory
Dichromat; acuity ~20/75; flicker threshold 70–80 Hz. Sees blue and yellow-green.
Motor
Quick, agile. Will produce the messiest, longest, multi-point nose drags in the house.
Motivation
Novelty, treat, your attention — in that order.
Goal
Someone to love who is not his mother.
Frustration
He touched it and nothing happened. Sound too quiet. Screen too far away.
Role in the process

Karmel tests input tolerance. If his swipe registers, anyone's will.

AuriThe user for whom this app has real value
Age, sex
12 years, female. Karmel's mother.
Sensory
Same dichromat baseline, but reduced contrast sensitivity from age-related changes in the lens.
Motor
Calmer. Longer pause before deciding, fewer trials per session.
Motivation
Routine, quiet, the human's presence, food.
Goal
Someone to love who is not her son.
Hidden value
Cognitive stimulation — precisely the population Vienna's “sudoku for dogs” was aimed at.
Role in the process

Auri sets the thresholds: minimum contrast, minimum target size, maximum session length.

KonstancjaOwner, operator, matchmaker — and the only one who understands why any of this exists
Tasks
Prepare the deck and profiles, run the session, sit alongside, reward, read the result, arrange the real meeting.
Goal
Two less bored dogs, one real meeting — and the satisfaction of a thing she built herself.
Need
Setup in minutes, not hours. A result legible at a glance. Silence once the session is over.
Constraint
She cannot be in two modes at once: while the dog works, she rewards — she does not tap.
Role in the process

She is the interpreter. The app hands her a signal; the decision stays hers.

Conclusion oneDesign for Auri, stress-test with Karmel

The two dogs share a dichromat baseline but not a contrast threshold. Every floor — contrast, target size, session length — is validated on the twelve-year-old. The younger dog then copes by definition, and his sloppy input is what proves the tolerance.

Conclusion twoThese are two interfaces, not one

Dog mode: full screen, no chrome, four-times scale, blue and yellow-green. Human mode: normal density, normal typography, legible data. They must be separated — and the dog must not be able to reach the second one.
