Tinder for Chihuahua · Service blueprint

The line of visibility

The journey says what happens. The blueprint says what has to work underneath for it to happen at all — and where the edge of the dog’s world actually is. Everything below the line of interaction is invisible to Karmel, and without it there is no session. The last row pairs each risk with the design decision that answers it.

Risk
and answer
The dog knocks the phone over.
→ an angled stand, and never a loose phone on the floor
The dog does not touch it at all.
→ a longer warm-up, the whole screen as target, a better treat
A wet nose registers three swipes instead of one.
→ a 1500 ms cooldown, the patch centroid, and undo as a core function
The dog goes looking for another card.
→ D5 with no acid and no “next”; the app never tops the deck up
Someone over-reads the ranking.
→ H1 says it in as many words: a preference signal, not a match
The dogs do not like each other despite the swipe.
→ expected. A picture is no substitute for a smell, which is exactly why the decision belongs to a human
